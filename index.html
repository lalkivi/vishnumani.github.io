Design Philosophy & Background

My fascination with biological locomotion originated as a child watching my mother prepare lecture notes and medical illustrations for her clinical teaching at a medical school. Observing how intricate anatomical systems coordinate to generate fluid human motion shaped my perspective on physical mechanics.

That curiosity converged with mechanical design when I took apart my first bicycle, discovering the back-pedal coaster brake mechanism. It was my first realization that a mechanical system's behavior is fundamentally dictated by its designer's architecture.

To unite these two domains, I traveled abroad to pursue robotics engineering, working directly on cutting-edge walking systems: rehabilitation exoskeletons for spinal cord injury mobility, augmentative exosuits for elderly assistance, and high-mobility humanoids at IHMC.

Humanoid Robotics

IHMC Humanoid Robot: NADIA

High-Mobility Hybrid Humanoid for Urban Disaster & Confined Operations

System Overview

IHMC developed Nadia as a next-generation humanoid robot designed for autonomous and semi-autonomous behaviors in urban environments and complex indoor structures where stairs, ladders, and uneven debris are present. Nadia requires human-equivalent range of motion (ROM) and torque characteristics to perform high-load behaviors such as walking, squatting, dynamic ladder ascension, and two-handed manipulation.

Distal Mass Reduction & Instrumented Carbon Fiber Feet

Locomotive agility in bipeds is heavily governed by distal mass (approximated by the Linear Inverted Pendulum model). To maximize stride frequency and minimize leg inertia, I led the effort to offload weight from the lower extremities.

  • Center of Pressure (COP) Estimation: Replaced heavy, expensive commercial 6-axis Force/Torque sensors at the feet with custom carbon-fiber foot plates instrumented with calibrated strain gauges.
  • Atlas Benchmark Characterization: Characterized the structural stiffness of the DARPA Robotics Challenge DRC Atlas robot feet to balance structural resilience with elastic shock absorption.

3-DOF Compact Torso Joint Architecture

Most humanoid torsos possess minimal degrees of freedom, preventing them from leaning down to lift objects or recovering after falls. I engineered a lightweight 3-DOF torso providing Pitch, Roll, and Yaw while minimizing swept volume to house the onboard Hydraulic Power Unit (HPU), computer stack, and batteries.

  • Zero-Backlash Push-Push Actuation: Designed a push-push dual hydraulic cylinder kinematic setup across the pitch and roll axes. Constant dual-side pressure completely eliminates joint backlash across the entire ROM and provides symmetrical torque output.
  • Kinematic Optimization: Formulated an optimization routine to minimize swept cylinder volume, resulting in precise mounting pivots and crank arm dimensions transferred directly into CAD.
  • FEA & Moment Load Testing: Evaluated cycloidal and planetary yaw drives under severe moment loading conditions (up to 300 Nm) to ensure structural factor-of-safety margins.

Custom Hydraulic Actuator Assembly & 3000 PSI Hydrostatic Testing

  • Contamination Control: Cleaned custom cylinder parts, manifolds, and rod seal pucks using ultrasonic cleaning baths and continuous low-pressure flushing to protect delicate servo-valve micro-orifices.
  • Dynamic Sealing: Integrated high-durability static and dynamic hydraulic seals on plunger rods.
  • Pressure Step Verification: Conducted stepped hydrostatic pressure testing from 100 PSI up to 3,000 PSI to verify structural integrity and zero external leakage, followed by cyclic stroke friction mapping.
Medical Exoskeleton

IHMC Team QUIX: Powered Mobility Exoskeleton

Cybathlon 2020 & Toyota Mobility Unlimited Challenge Finalist Platform

Overview & Achievement

IHMC Quix is a powered 8-actuator lower-limb medical exoskeleton engineered for individuals with complete spinal cord injuries (SCI). The device competed in the global 2020 Cybathlon and Toyota Mobility Unlimited Challenge, placing in the top tiers worldwide.

16-Sensor Center of Pressure (COP) Sole

To supply the control architecture with real-time foot contact states, I developed custom sensorized footplates:

  • Urethane Rubber Encapsulation: Developed precision liquid urethane molding processes to securely pot pressure-sensitive elements into rugged soles.
  • 16-Sensor Sensor Array: Designed and calibrated a 16-sensor distributed matrix across heel, arch, metatarsal, and toe regions.
  • Real-Time Visualization: Engineered communication and data streaming protocols to visualize 2D COP trajectory during human pilot walking.

Active Cooling Thermal Backpack & Quick-Swap Battery

  • SolidWorks CFD Airflow: Conducted internal thermal fluid flow simulations to optimize inlet and outlet ventilation ports on the electronic backpack, integrating active forced-air cooling fans with conductive aluminum baseplate heat dissipation.
  • Leaf-Spring Battery Mount: Designed a four-sided leaf-spring locking mechanism enabling quick-swap battery replacement during competition maneuvers, functioning seamlessly like an industrial drill battery interface.

Carbon Fiber Thigh Cuffs (3-6-3 Layup)

Exoskeleton cuffs must safely distribute high interaction forces across paralyzed limbs without creating localized pressure ulcers:

  • Engineered three-part modular tooling molds to manufacture oversized composite thigh cuffs.
  • Formulated a customized 3-6-3 mono-6-mono pre-preg carbon fiber layup schedule to achieve optimal directional stiffness and compliance.
  • Finished edges with tailored rubber edging and softgood padding to prevent tissue shearing.
MS Thesis Research • WPI

Quasi-Passive Wrap Spring Clutch Knee Joint (LARRE)

Published in ASME IDETC-CIE 2020 & Master's Thesis at Worcester Polytechnic Institute

Research Objective

Standard powered exoskeleton knee joints demand heavy gearboxes and high continuous electrical power to support body weight during stance. My thesis designed, manufactured, and experimentally validated a quasi-passive knee joint based on a wrap-spring clutch/brake architecture capable of mechanically locking under stance loads with near-zero electrical power consumption.

Engineering & Manufacturing Execution

  • Mechanism Trade Study: Benchmarked magneto-rheological fluids, pawl-ratchet drives, friction plates, and wrap-spring clutches across weight, cost, parasitic power, and holding torque.
  • High-Tolerance Arbor Machining: Programmed CAM toolpaths in ESPRIT and CNC-machined custom steel arbor drums with micron-level cylindrical tolerances and surface finishes to ensure repeatable friction engagement.
  • Dynamic Dynamometer Characterization: Tested full-scale assemblies on a custom test bench to map engagement latency, release torque, holding capacity under human-equivalent bodyweight moments, and fatigue life.

Propulsion & Biomechanics

Cold Gas Propulsion Jump Augmentation System

Biomechanical Modeling, Supersonic Nozzle CFD, and Hardware Fabrication

Concept & Abaqus Dynamic Simulation

This self-funded R&D project investigated whether human vertical jump height could be dramatically augmented using an impulsive wearable cold-gas thruster:

  • Abaqus Spring-Mass Dynamic Model: Built an analytical spring-mass human drop-jump simulation in Abaqus. Instead of applying conventional constant force, the model evaluated an impulsive force pulse injected strictly at the mechanical rebound phase of the jump.
  • Force Timing: Proved analytically that delivering a targeted impulse during positive center-of-mass acceleration yields maximum vertical velocity with minimum propulsive mass.

De Laval Convergent-Divergent Nozzle Design

  • Gas Dynamics: Derived analytical equations for a supersonic De Laval nozzle to maximize exit velocity and thrust-to-weight ratio.
  • SolidWorks Compressible Flow CFD: Ran CFD flow simulations to verify Mach number distributions, expansion shockwaves, and mass flow rates against analytical equations.
  • CNC Lathe Manufacturing: Precision-machined the nozzle on a CNC lathe. Empirical testing revealed flow limitations within commercial regulators, defining future specifications for custom high-flow pneumatic valves.
Aerospace Engineering

Variable Damping Nose Landing Gear

BS Capstone: Non-Linear Hydraulic Damping for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles

Problem Statement

Standard fixed-orifice oleo-pneumatic dampers in UAV nose landing gear exhibit sluggish settling times, high peak rebound forces, and non-critical damping ratios across fluctuating touchdown velocities.

Tapered Metering Pin Mechanism

  • Tapered Variable Orifice: Engineered a variable-damping oleo-pneumatic strut featuring a tapered metering pin that continuously modifies the fluid orifice area as a function of piston stroke.
  • Nonlinear MATLAB Simulation: Modeled the dynamic fluid equations, pressure differentials, and discharge coefficients in MATLAB to tune the pin taper geometry for near-critical damping.
  • Abaqus 1D Dynamic Shock Modeling: Validated the mathematical model through Abaqus impact simulations, demonstrating faster settling times and reduced peak forces compared to conventional constant dampers.
Electromechanical Actuation

Dual Stator Dual Rotor (DSDR) Electric Motor

Quasi-Direct Drive (QDD) High-Torque-Density Actuator Architecture

Motivation

High-ratio gearboxes add excessive reflected inertia, mechanical backlash, and friction, severely degrading open-loop torque control and backdrivability in legged robots. Direct-drive and QDD motors solve this but typically suffer from low torque output per unit mass.

Topology & Prototyping

  • Investigated concentric dual-stator dual-rotor configurations (combining internal and external air gaps) to double effective electromagnetic shear area.
  • Evaluated both inrunner and outrunner topologies for thermal conduction paths and torque output.
  • Built experimental prototypes to benchmark open-loop torque control bandwidth and thermal saturation thresholds for robot joints.
Novel Actuators

Dual-Pole Solenoid & Voice Coil Actuator

Direct Linear Drive with Controlled Electromagnetic Compliance

Design & Investigation

Rotary-to-linear linkages add unwanted mechanical mass and complexity to robotic joints. This project investigated whether a customized dual-pole solenoid configuration could achieve controllable dynamic response with reduced thermal penalties.

  • L298N Drive Setup: Designed an experimental testbed driven by an L298N H-bridge controller running dual-speed and bidirectional current pulses.
  • Thermal Dissipation: Demonstrated that split dual-pole winding geometries drastically reduced steady-state heat generation compared to standard single-coil solenoids.
  • Virtual Spring Dynamics: Programmed closed-loop position states that exhibited tunable spring-like elastic compliance for robotic foot impact absorption.
Wearable Systems

Ankle & Hip Compliant Assistive Exosuit

Low-Cost, Non-AI Gait-Synchronized Assistive Exoskeleton

Hardware & Control Strategy

Most modern exoskeletons rely on complex, computationally heavy AI frameworks and expensive multi-sensor arrays. This prototype proved that effective biological gait assistance can be achieved with lightweight, cost-effective hardware:

  • MJBots Brushless Drive: Integrated an ultra-compact MJBots brushless motor and high-resolution driver coupled to an ankle pulley via high-tensile Bowden cable transmission.
  • Heel Contact Timing: Deployed two robust pressure switches across the heel strike zone to detect midstance transition mechanically, eliminating algorithmic latency.
  • Variable Stiffness Emulation: The motor acts as a high-stiffness virtual spring delivering up to 70 N of assistive plantarflexion torque during terminal stance, maintaining minimal background cable tension during swing.
Dynamic Simulation

Human Gait Kinematics & Dynamic Simulation

Vicon Motion Tracking, Multi-Segment IMUs, and Python Biomechanical Models

Project Objective

Developed a high-fidelity dynamic simulation of human walking by capturing, filtering, and modeling experimental biomechanical data across human subjects.

Experimental Methodology

  • Contoured IMU Mounting Brackets: Designed and 3D-printed custom, anatomically contoured limb brackets that conform securely to various thigh and shank profiles via slotted Velcro straps, eliminating sensor slippage artifacts.
  • Sensor Fusion: Recorded gait cycles using 20 Vicon optical markers tracked by high-speed infrared cameras synchronized with 7 body-mounted IMUs.
  • Dynamic Modeling: Post-processed kinematic trajectory and ground reaction data in Python and Vicon Nexus to drive dynamic multi-link walking simulations.
Control Systems

Two-Wheeled Self-Balancing Inverted Pendulum

State Estimation, IMU Filtering, and Disturbance Rejection

System Overview

Engineered a standalone two-wheeled inverted pendulum testbed from the ground up to validate real-time state estimation algorithms and closed-loop balance stabilization.

  • IMU Sensor Fusion: Combined accelerometer and gyroscopic rate signals through complementary filtering to obtain zero-drift tilt angle calculations under dynamic platform accelerations.
  • PID Parameter Tuning: Tuned proportional, integral, and derivative gain matrices to balance rapid impulse disturbance rejection with minimum actuator saturation and chattering.
Control Systems

PID Closed-Loop Velocity & Dynamic Wheelie Control

Precision Encoder Feedback, Transient Response Tuning, and Equilibrium Inversion

Objective & Control Strategy

Designed a custom dual-motor robotic platform to analyze non-linear transient dynamics under closed-loop velocity and torque feedback:

  • Encoder Feedback Loop: Implemented real-time quadrature encoder tracking to govern closed-loop velocity control across varied rolling friction surfaces.
  • Dynamic Wheelie Mode: Tuned torque command injection at the drive wheels to intentionally pop up into a balanced wheelie state, evaluating equilibrium recovery under aggressive step inputs.
